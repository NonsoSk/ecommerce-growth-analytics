# Measurement plan — synthetic Milestone 1

**In development.** This is a measurement contract for a fictional mattress retailer, not findings about Puffy or real customers. The question is where journeys lose potential buyers, how acquisition performance varies, and which issues merit investigation or experiments. Milestone 2 implements local synthetic SQL analysis under these rules; it does not establish real recommendations or measured uplift. See MEASUREMENT_REVIEW.md for reviewed clarifications.

## Shared rules

- Default cohort: sessions starting in **[2026-01-01 00:00:00Z, 2026-04-01 00:00:00Z)**, exactly 90 days. Alternative starts retain 90 days. Use UTC and USD; monetary inputs are integer cents. Convert to dollars only for presentation.
- Default reporting grain: one UTC session-start day, channel and device. Paid campaign reporting can add campaign ID. Totals must be recomputed from numerators and denominators, never averages of daily ratios. CAC and ROAS have the coarser grain specified below.
- A session is an explicit synthetic ID, not a reconstruction using a real analytics timeout. Steps are ordered within a session. A visitor can have many sessions; a customer can have many orders. A purchase event is not itself an order or a distinct customer.
- Eligible orders are valid `paid` ledger orders linked to included sessions with matching customer IDs and unique transaction IDs. Financial reconciliation checks item amounts, charges and refunds independently of purchase-event delivery. Missing tracking does not erase a valid order. The event funnel additionally requires an accepted event matching the order transaction, session and occurrence time. Full/partial refunds do not erase a purchase. No failed payments, cancellations, bots, staff traffic, consent loss or fraud are generated; real implementations must identify and exclude ineligible traffic and unpaid/cancelled/test orders explicitly.
- At analysis time, select event records by `ingested_at < cutoff` and report them by **occurrence**, not ingestion date. Default cutoff is start + 105 days (2026-04-16 exclusive). This gives the last cohort day time for the simulated 1–14-day refunds. Refunds use both `refunded_at < cutoff` and `ingested_at < cutoff`. Do not silently treat immature cohorts as final.
- Include only sessions ended before cutoff and orders occurring before cutoff. Session metrics use session-start cohort dates; event occurrence determines within-session ordering. Mark future daily rows not_yet_observed and flag sessions with less than 14 days plus five minutes of refund follow-up. Orders and spend lack ingestion timestamps, so early cutoffs are not complete historical warehouse snapshots.
- Refund-adjusted metrics below use **order-cohort accounting**: subtract refunds from the originating order/session cohort, even if refunded after the 90-day traffic period. This is not a cash-flow report of refunds paid on each calendar day. Tax and shipping refunds are recorded separately and excluded from merchandise revenue.
- A zero denominator produces null / “not available,” never zero or infinity. Show denominators with rates and disclose small samples. Do not imply population precision from simulation.

## Metric definitions

| Metric | Numerator | Denominator | Grain and time window | Exclusions / interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase conversion rate (order-based) | Distinct eligible sessions with at least one valid paid ledger order | All eligible complete sessions | Session-start day × channel × device; roll up over the 90-day session cohort | Count a buying session once even if it has multiple orders. Missing/unmatched purchase events are tracking issues reported separately, not grounds to delete valid ledger orders. Unmatched event-only purchases do not create sales. Refunds do not remove conversions. |
| Step-by-step funnel conversion | Distinct sessions reaching next step **after** the current step, with the ordered prefix satisfied | Distinct sessions reaching current step with its ordered prefix satisfied | Same session cohort/grain; session_start → view_item → add_to_cart → begin_checkout → purchase | One count per session per step; repeated events cannot inflate counts. Purchase requires a matching eligible order. Missing or out-of-order steps are reported separately and excluded from the strict prefix numerator. Loss count = denominator − numerator; loss rate = 1 − step conversion. |
| Average order value (AOV) | Sum of gross merchandise less discounts on eligible orders, **before refunds** | Distinct eligible orders | Session-start day × channel × device, 90-day session cohort; assign each order to its session | Excludes tax, shipping and unpaid/cancelled/test orders. Refunded orders stay in the denominator. This is explicitly pre-refund merchandise AOV; do not label it net AOV. |
| Net merchandise revenue | Gross merchandise − discounts − eligible merchandise refunds for cohort orders | None (additive currency amount) | Session-start day × channel × device; 90-day cohort observed through cutoff | Excludes tax, shipping and their refunds. Do not subtract total refund cash including tax from merchandise revenue. |
| Revenue per session | Net merchandise revenue, as defined above, for cohort sessions | All eligible sessions, including nonbuyers | Same session cohort/grain and refund cutoff | Uses net merchandise revenue, not platform-attributed gross sales. Shows revenue per visit, not per customer. |
| Customer acquisition cost (paid media CAC proxy) | Sum of paid media spend at campaign-day grain | Distinct first-observed purchasing customers whose first paid order is in an included session attributed to that campaign/channel | UTC day × paid channel × campaign, or full 90-day rollup. First order is selected globally by ordered_at then order_id before grouping | Includes zero-conversion spend days. Excludes organic/direct from paid CAC; do not call their CAC zero. Excludes salaries, creative, agency and other acquisition costs, so this is not fully loaded CAC. “New” means first observed in this synthetic history, not proven lifetime-new. |
| Return on ad spend (ROAS) | Net merchandise revenue of eligible orders attributed to paid sessions | Spend for the same campaign-day cohorts | UTC session-start day × paid channel × campaign; 90-day totals; refunds through cutoff | Includes spend days without sales. Not profit: product costs, overhead and nonmedia costs are absent. This project’s net ROAS can differ from ad-platform gross ROAS. |

## Attribution, spend and causal limits

The explicit rule is **same-session attribution**. Each order inherits exactly one session's channel/campaign; direct stays direct, with no lookback or last-nondirect carryover. Repeat purchases receive their own purchase-session attribution. A new customer's first order determines its single CAC acquisition assignment; later orders contribute revenue but not new-customer counts. These are modeling choices, not claims about platform attribution. Direct is not attributed to a paid campaign under this model and may include missing attribution or prior paid exposure. Organic does not imply zero acquisition cost; content, SEO and other costs are not supplied.

Aggregate order items and refunds to one row per order first. Link orders to sessions (many orders to one session), then aggregate attributed revenue and first-observed purchasers to campaign-day. Separately aggregate spend at its existing campaign-day key. Combine using the **union of spend and outcome keys** so spend-only and conversion-only days remain. Missing spend is null, not zero; campaign summary ratios are suppressed if spend coverage is incomplete. **Never join daily spend to individual events, sessions or order items and then sum it.** Spend cannot be broken down by device from this schema; report device conversion/revenue separately, not device CAC or ROAS with duplicated costs.

Observed channel differences do not identify causal impact. Device/channel mix, repeat behavior and the simulation's hard-coded probabilities determine the patterns. No experiment was run, no successful intervention is simulated, and no revenue improvement is claimed. Future experiments require a hypothesis, randomization plan, primary metric, guardrails, sample-size reasoning and a decision rule.

## Data-quality handling

See [QUALITY_FIXTURES.md](QUALITY_FIXTURES.md). Keep raw records; validate purchase links and chronology, then deduplicate valid purchase events by transaction ID (earliest ingestion then event ID). Invalid earlier records cannot suppress later valid matches. Quarantine missing IDs rather than guessing a match. Conflicting payloads require review. Late arrivals remain valid if otherwise consistent; restate their occurrence cohort after ingestion, with an explicit as-of cutoff. Local SQLite audit, exclusion and rerun handling are implemented; a production quarantine/backfill service is not.
