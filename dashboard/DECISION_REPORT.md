# Synthetic demonstration: proposed investigations

These are prioritized hypotheses for learning how to investigate a funnel, not findings about real customers. The simulation deliberately encodes behavior. No intervention was run, and no causal effect or revenue improvement is claimed. Before commercial action, repeat measurement and reconciliation independently on appropriately authorized real data.

| Priority | Observation in the simulation | Uncertainty and alternative explanation | Testable change to consider after evidence review | Success metric and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Product-page progression | 2,380 ordered product-view sessions become 1,036 cart sessions: 43.53% progression, 1,344 drop-offs. | The generator controls these transitions. In real data, comparison shopping, price, missing events or cross-session journeys could explain the loss. | Hypothesis: clearer total price, delivery and returns information improves qualified cart progression. First audit event sequencing and review consented usability sessions. | Predefine cart/view conversion and purchase/session conversion, with net revenue/session and refund guardrails. Require validated real tracking, usability evidence, random assignment and a justified sample-size/time plan before an experiment. |
| 2 — Comparable mobile journeys | Mobile ordered purchase/session: 216/1,833 = 11.78%; desktop 161/867 = 18.57%. | Device behavior is simulated. Source, visit cohort, intent, identity loss and cross-device journeys may explain an observed real-world gap. | Hypothesis: removing verified mobile interaction friction improves progression. First compare device results within comparable source and observed-visit cohorts, then inspect affected journeys. | Within-cohort ordered funnel and purchase/session conversion; guardrails for net revenue, errors and refunds. Require real device diagnostics, tracking parity and controlled testing before changing experience. |
| 3 — Attribution before spend changes | 195 of 360 campaign days have spend and zero attributed orders; full paid-attributed net ROAS is 3.91. | Same-session attribution can miss delayed/cross-device effects. Sparse daily conversions and the simulation's allocation also produce zero-order days. Attributed ROAS is not incremental ROAS. | Hypothesis: some apparent low-performing days reflect the attribution window. Compare reconciled same-session and defensible longer-window assignments before proposing spend changes. | Transaction reconciliation, unattributed share and stability across attribution windows; causal value requires an adequately designed lift/holdout study. Require authorized real spend, click/session and order data with documented consent/identity limits. |

Landing-page analysis remains blocked by the source schema: no landing-page field exists. Instrumentation and independent real-data analysis remain backlog items; no source values should be invented to complete a chart.
